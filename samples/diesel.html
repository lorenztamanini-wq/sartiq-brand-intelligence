Brand Brief

Diesel

3.3/5 Denim-led, youth, repositioned as 'the true alternative to luxury'
Parent OTB Group (Diesel, Margiela, Marni, Jil Sander, Viktor&Rolf + Brave Kid)  ·  Markets Global; flagships opening (Berlin, Seoul); ~600+ stores (group)
Partners Maison Margiela, Marni, Jil Sander, Viktor&Rolf, Brave Kid licensees (Diesel Kid, Dsquared², Marni, N21, Trussardi), Amiri (OTB strategic stake), wholesale partners
1Who
Diesel is a denim-led, youth-oriented Italian lifestyle brand (founded 1978 by Renzo Rosso), now deliberately positioned as "the true alternative to luxury" — accessible-luxury tier (core jeans ~$195–295, premium pieces up to ~$1,895). It sits inside OTB Group alongside Maison Margiela, Jil Sander, Marni and Viktor&Rolf, plus Staff International and Brave Kid, and an Amiri stake. Creative direction by Glenn Martens (since Oct 2020); Andrea Rigogliosi appointed brand CEO Jan 2026. Global footprint (OTB ~600 owned stores; Japan ~27% of group sales, growing NA +5.9% and Middle East +9%); channel mix is DTC-growth-led with a still-significant but shrinking wholesale channel (group wholesale down ~15%, target 25–30% of turnover). Range spans apparel, accessories, footwear and licensed lines (fragrances, watches, Diesel Living).

2Directionest
INNOVATING (at brand level). Since Glenn Martens' arrival (Oct 2020) Diesel has run a visible repositioning: heritage low-rise/distressed-denim packshot heritage gave way to bold, youth-led, campaign-driven imagery, public livestreamed runway shows and a digital-native engagement strategy. Live PDP imagery now reads as a deliberately premium, ultra-consistent on-model system (~96% on-model on clean white, high consistency — see q5). Triggers in play: brand-CEO change (Andrea Rigogliosi, Jan 2026), flagship expansion (Berlin, Seoul Hannam), new-market entries (Mexico, Qatar/Kuwait JV). Net read: Diesel is innovating and investing, not copying. By contrast the parent OTB group is in efficiency mode (wholesale bleeding, write-downs from creative-director changes at sister maisons). The gap we fill ░ human ░ ✓ confirmed

Diesel and OTB need OPPOSITE things from the same supplier — and no single vendor is currently serving both registers. The Diesel brand is winning (best profitability in a decade, youth-led, Berlin/Seoul flagships, new market entries) and is buying INNOVATION: distinctive AI on-model imagery to feed its digital-native DTC push, localized at near-zero marginal cost across Japan (~27% of group sales), NA (+5.9%) and Middle East (+9%). The OTB parent is buying EFFICIENCY: operating profit collapsed ~77%, wholesale down ~15%, margins hit by write-downs from new creative directors at Margiela/Jil Sander/Marni. The concrete wedge: Diesel's own PDPs are near-perfectly consistent (95.8% on-model, plain white, ~4 shots/SKU), but that coherence does NOT travel to the wholesale/marketplace channel (Farfetch et al.) or sister maisons. One partner plays both: premium AI imagery for the brand, commodity-cost consistency for the group's maisons, Brave Kid licensees, and a still-large but shrinking wholesale channel that needs cheap, on-brand assets.

Strategy ░ human ░ ✓ confirmed

DUAL — say the split out loud; do not average it into a 'stable' story. Thread 1 to the Diesel brand digital/e-comm lead: INNOVATION — distinctive AI on-model imagery plus near-zero-cost localization to sustain a winning, Gen-Z, digital-native DTC trajectory through the Rigogliosi CEO transition and flagship expansion (a live content-surge trigger). Thread 2 to OTB central digital: EFFICIENCY — consistent, on-brand assets at commodity cost to relieve margin pressure and re-arm the shrinking wholesale channel and sister maisons/licensees. The two audiences hear different things by design. Anchor both on OTB's own public AI/clienteling/Google Cloud virtual-try-on investments as proof they are already buying AI tooling.
